# Squares Plan & Backlog

The living content backlog for BINGOPE squares: what territory exists, what we've mined, and which ideas were parked or killed. This is *what to write about*. For *how to write* it, see [`writing-style.md`](writing-style.md). Approved squares live under [`src/ts/partials/squares/`](../src/ts/partials/squares/) (`_pool`, `_centers`, `_essentials`, re-exported by [`src/ts/partials/_squares.ts`](../src/ts/partials/_squares.ts)) — those files are the source of truth for what is "done," so it is not duplicated here.

## Area coverage

Territory to draw squares from. Status is coverage, not quality.

- ✅ **Tapped** — at least one square banked from it
- 🔷 **Open** — unmined, fair game
- ❌ **Closed** — mined and nothing landed. Deliberately not revisiting.

The issue #16 completion pass closed every row it could. The **scissors pass** that followed (see the log below) then cut two banked squares on quality grounds, which reopened Big Fat Bacon and closed Malts outright, so two rows moved after that pass. New rows should only appear if genuinely new territory is identified, the way Weather, Food sticker shock, Phones & vaping, and Live animal judging were added mid-pass after the inherited table turned out to be incomplete.

## The scissors pass

A review of the banked pool for its *weakest* squares, run before merging the completion pass. Twelve candidates were nominated and adjudicated one by one. The result was 2 cuts, 6 rewrites, 1 new square, and 5 explicit keeps.

**The five keeps matter as much as the cuts.** Each was nominated and defended, so do not re-pitch cutting them.

| Square | Nominated because | Kept because |
|---|---|---|
| P28, hog over 270 lbs | 'I was certain' is a guess with no scale in sight | The weight is properly researched and the claim is honestly made. Totally claimable. |
| P36, coin-pusher tickets | Outcome is not in the player's control and there is no joke | **The odds are the joke.** Coin pushers pay out almost never, so actually walking away with tickets is itself the remarkable event. |
| P30, new-foods list | Procedural homework, and the payoff is hedged away | The homework is the point. It rewards being a *proper* fairgoer, and the annual new-foods list is a real fair institution. |
| P43, queen bee | The 'with help if needed' hedge deletes the challenge | Happy with it as-is. |
| P48, prize wheel | The flattest square in the pool | The *action* is good even if the writing was not. Kept the action, rewrote the line. |

The two cuts were **Big Fat Bacon's fake share** (reopening that row) and the **Dairy Building malt** (closing that row). Everything else nominated was repaired rather than removed.

Three failure modes did most of the work, and they are worth checking new drafts against.

- **Surveillance masquerading as observation.** If marking a square means following one stranger across several minutes or several trips (the 4th milk refill, the five-minute cotton candy watch, a whole offer-and-renege exchange), it is not a sighting. Look for the *artifact* the event leaves behind and mark that instead.
- **Inner-state reads that survived earlier review.** 'Realize in real time', 'looking really annoyed', 'damp and disappointed'. The style guide already bans these, and three still shipped, so audit for them specifically rather than trusting the ban.
- **Vague comparators.** 'The size of a pillow' fails because pillows vary. 'Bigger than their own head' works because heads do not, and it self-calibrates to whoever is carrying the thing.

## Unexplored technique: grandiose framing

Surfaced while writing P77 ('I witnessed the death of a balloon'). The style guide's core rule is **specificity is the joke**, and that holds for sightings. But P77 works the opposite way: it is deliberately *non*-specific, and the humor comes from describing something trivial in weighty, formal language. It stays markable because a popped balloon is unmistakable, so vagueness costs nothing.

This is a distinct lever from specificity and it is **largely unexplored across the pool**. Worth a pass to see which other mundane, guaranteed fair events can be elevated the same way. Candidates to test the technique on, not yet drafted or approved. If it proves out on more than one square, promote it into [`writing-style.md`](writing-style.md) as a named technique alongside 'specificity is the joke'.

## Verified reference: real animal names

Researched during the issue #16 completion pass. The fair publishes **per-breed entry lists** at <https://www.mnstatefair.org/competitions/entries/>, with columns `# | Registration # | Date of Birth | Animal Name | Exhibitor Name | Exhibitor Farm | City, State | Sire Name | Dam Name`. 2,767 individual 2026 animal names were scraped across 16 breed pages.

- **Human first names are abundant (~100 hits).** Including a real beef entry named **`Betty White`**, plus `WILDCARD R DAMMIT RANDY`, `Rising-Heits Meet Virginia`, `Shirley`, `Pam`, `Stella`, `Victor`, `Mabel`. The skew is mid-century feminine. **`Kevin`, `Brenda`, `Doug`, `Gary`, and `Karen` appear zero times**, so never name a specific example in a square.
- **Food names are plentiful.** `Pickles`, `Coconut`, `Kiwi`, `Pepper`, `Noodle`, `Apple Pie`, `Banana Split`, `Lemon Drop`, `Skittles`, `Little Miss Snickers`, `Popcorn`, `Chilly Pumpkin`, `Raspberry Truffle`. No `Biscuit`, no `Meatball`.
- **Species matters enormously.** Llamas (292 entries) and Boer goats carry simple one-word names (`Bert`, `Ernie`, `Kramer`, `Trigger`, `Stanley`, `Stella`, `George`, `Bobby`, `Jenny`). Holsteins and Ayrshires have essentially none, since dairy registered names are farm-prefix soup (`BOUGIE REDHOT GREENY-RED-ET`). **Any name-hunting square should point players at the llama and goat barns.**

### What is actually visible in the barns

All four 2026 premium books ([beef](https://assets.mnstatefair.org/pdf/competition/26-livestock-beef-pb.pdf), [dairy](https://assets.mnstatefair.org/pdf/competition/26-livestock-dairy-pb.pdf), [sheep](https://assets.mnstatefair.org/pdf/competition/26-livestock-sheep-pb.pdf), [poultry/rabbit](https://assets.mnstatefair.org/pdf/competition/26-livestock-poultry-pb.pdf)) were text-extracted and searched for 'stall card', 'pen card', 'cage card', 'placard', and 'signage'.

**Open class signage is exhibitor-supplied farm advertising.** Beef rule 15, verbatim: *"No signs of any type will be permitted in the barns, except within the specified assigned stall or on the bulletin board by the superintendent's office. A. Advertising of anything other than the specific farm on display is a violation…"* Dairy rule 16 is near-identical. Poultry rule 12 allows one 5x7 advertising card on a coop, but only after judging concludes.

✅ **This means farm name and hometown are reliably visible**, since that is what a farm advertises about itself. An earlier draft of this doc flagged P66 as at-risk by misapplying the **4-H** premium book's *"exhibitor name and county"* stall-sign rule to the whole fairgrounds. That rule governs the 4-H barns only.

**P66 was rewritten to Otter Tail anyway, and is now signage-proof.** The original said 'a cow from Clarissa' (a city in Todd County), which only worked if a hometown was displayed. **Ottertail is both a real city and the county it sits in**, so 'a cow from Otter Tail' is markable whether the sign shows a hometown (open class farm advertising) or a county (4-H). The trailing 'It explained nothing.' was cut, since the flat statement lands harder.

⚠️ **Still unresolved: whether the animal's own name is posted.** No rule in any of the four books provides a fair-issued stall card or specifies its contents. The books are exhaustive about ear-tag formats and tie-out fees, so the silence is suggestive but is not proof either way.

**Parked as in-person homework, not a blocker.** Lemon is at the fair roughly 2026-08-30 and will check the pens then. Until that answer exists, these three stay unwritten:

1. **'Find the cow named Betty White'** — a real 2026 beef entry, and the single best name on the list. Wanted badly.
2. **An animal named after a food** — verified plentiful (`Pickles`, `Apple Pie`, `Banana Split`, `Skittles`, `Little Miss Snickers`). Must stay categorical, since no specific food repeats year to year.
3. **An animal with an ordinary human first name** — ~100 real hits, but never name a specific one. `Kevin`, `Brenda`, `Doug`, `Gary`, and `Karen` all score zero across 2,767 names.

If names turn out to be readable, point all three at the **llama and Boer goat barns** (76 of 292 llama entries are bare one-word names). If not, close the avenue.
